# Faculty-fit map for targeted thesis outreach

**Status:** research-alignment map, not evidence of current openings or willingness to supervise  
**Verified:** 2026-08-24 from official university or faculty pages

U of T's Robotics Institute advises applicants to read recent lab work, write a short first email, explain why the specific lab is right, and state what they bring. The Robotics Institute itself is not degree-granting; the applicant must apply through the professor's home department. This map supports that tailoring. It should never be pasted wholesale into an email.

## Highest direct alignment

### Florian Shkurti — University of Toronto, Robot Vision and Learning Lab

**Why the fit is unusually direct:** his stated interests include policy evaluation, risk estimation, uncertainty quantification, safe robot learning, rare-event generation, and video/sensor data summarization. Those overlap the proposal's decision-level evaluation, shift stress tests, and abstention logic.

**Pitch angle:** a statistically valid evaluation layer for learned robot policies, especially when video-based reward models fail heterogeneously across task conditions.

**Do not claim:** that the project certifies deployment safety or that he is currently accepting students. His page contained 2025-2026 recruiting language; availability must be rechecked before contact.

**Sources:** <https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~florian/> and <https://www.utm.utoronto.ca/math-cs-stats/people/florian-shkurti>

### Nicholas Rhinehart — University of Toronto, LEAF Lab / UTIAS

**Why the fit is strong:** LEAF focuses on reliable model-based robot autonomy, learning transferable models, and inferring objectives from sparse human or language supervision. His 2026 research program emphasizes failure under distribution change and grounded value models.

**Pitch angle:** evaluation certificates as a reliability layer for learned value/reward models, with explicit shift-triggered abstention and sparse human supervision.

**Risk:** the current project evaluates policies rather than learning world models or controllers. The outreach must explain the scientific connection rather than pretending they are the same problem.

**Sources:** <https://robotics.utoronto.ca/news/nick-rhinehart-receives-u-of-t-connaught-new-researcher-award-for-advancing-reliable-robot-autonomy/> and <https://robotics.utoronto.ca/event/robotics-institute-seminar-nick-rhinehart-u-of-t-2/>

## Strong methodological alignment

### Stephen L. Smith — University of Waterloo, Autonomous Systems Laboratory

**Why the fit is plausible:** ASL combines optimization, control, and machine learning with an emphasis on systems operating reliably in the real world. The lab works on learning-based planning/control and human-autonomy interaction.

**Pitch angle:** rigorous evidence for choosing among learned policies before scarce physical trials are committed; connect the certificate to dependable experimental decisions, not to control-theoretic safety guarantees.

**Risk:** policy evaluation is adjacent to, rather than central in, the lab's stated program. A tailored email should connect to a recent ASL paper or active project before contact.

**Sources:** <https://uwaterloo.ca/autonomous-systems-lab> and <https://uwaterloo.ca/robohub/profile/sl2smith>

### Krzysztof Czarnecki — University of Waterloo, WISE Lab

**Why the fit is plausible:** WISE studies assurance for ML-based autonomous systems, perceptual uncertainty, verification, validation, falsification, and statistical evidence supporting assurance claims.

**Pitch angle:** an explicit assurance case for when automated evaluation evidence is admissible, when it must abstain, and which guarantees are not provided.

**Risk:** WISE's core application has been automated driving and system assurance, not general robot manipulation evaluation. This may be a better methodological or co-supervision fit than a standalone manipulation thesis fit.

**Sources:** <https://uwaterloo.ca/waterloo-intelligent-systems-engineering-lab/projects/assuredai-safety-assurance-ai-based-automated-driving> and <https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/news/jo-atlee-krzysztof-czarnecki-students-receive-ten-year-most-influential-paper-award-vamos-2023>

## Conditional alignment; use carefully

### Mark Crowley — University of Waterloo, Machine Learning Lab

**Why the research fit exists:** his official page emphasizes dependable and transparent augmentation of human decision-making under uncertainty, reinforcement learning, and robotics/autonomous-systems applications.

**Outreach constraint:** his page explicitly says he rarely accepts unsolicited graduate applicants except through prior interaction, courses, conferences, or referrals. A generic cold email is therefore a poor strategy.

**Source:** <https://uwaterloo.ca/scholar/mcrowley>

## Outreach decision rule

Contact only when all five are true:

1. the professor's current appointment and recruiting instructions were rechecked that week;
2. at least one recent paper or active project has been read beyond the abstract;
3. the email states one specific overlap and one non-overlap honestly;
4. the applicant can state what they personally implemented and what remains unproved; and
5. the brief, CV, transcript, repository, and reference plan are ready.

## Recommended priority

1. **Florian Shkurti:** clearest direct match to policy evaluation and uncertainty.
2. **Nicholas Rhinehart:** strong match to reliable learned objectives and shift, if framed carefully.
3. **Stephen L. Smith:** strong reliable-autonomy environment; requires a more specific experimental-decision connection.
4. **Krzysztof Czarnecki:** strong assurance methodology; potentially strongest as co-supervision or an autonomous-systems assurance framing.
5. **Mark Crowley:** intellectual fit, but his published outreach constraint makes unsolicited contact low probability.

This ordering is about stated research alignment, not prestige, admission probability, funding, or current capacity.
